The Problem: Why One-Payment Checks Fail

Sourced Evidence

◆ Massive Scale & Fast Laundering

  • UPI Volume: 24.07 billion transactions worth Rs 29.37 lakh crore in September 2026 (DD India / NPCI).
  • Cyber Fraud Scale: Rs 10,178.97 crore reported lost across 12.71 lakh complaints (Jan–Jun 2026, MHA).
  • Low Recovery: Only Rs 2,968.85 crore (29%) held or frozen before exit (Outlook Money).
  • The Gap: Illicit money hops through chains of borrowed accounts within 5–20 minutes.

◆ The Structural Blindspot

  • Single-Payment Blindspot: Individual payments look ordinary (e.g. ₹48,000 P2P transfers).
  • Victims as Mules: Account holders are frequently duped students or jobseekers; automatic account freezing causes severe harm.
  • Industry Context: Public reports mention graph initiatives like RBI MuleHunter.AI; we do not claim parity with MuleHunter and have not compared implementations.
Key Insight: Once cash is withdrawn at an ATM or agent, recovery drops to near zero. Detection must occur at the relay stage.
Speaker Notes (0:45 - 1:30): In India, Rs 10,000+ crore was reported lost in just 6 months. Single-payment classifiers fail because individual transactions look normal. Detecting mule rings requires maintaining a rolling view of money flow topology as it forms.

System Architecture: Streaming Graph Engine

Technical Stack

◆ Pipeline Stages

  • 1. Event Ingestion: In-order streaming ingestion with bounded FIFO money-matching deques.
  • 2. 59 Streaming Features: 13 transaction, 37 account history, and 9 multi-account graph features (trail depth, funnel fan-in, neighbor suspicion).
  • 3. Portable GBDT: Exported JSON trees evaluated by pure-Python tree walker (zero C-extension dependencies).
  • 4. Union-Find Ring Grouping: Streaming union-find over flagged edges clustering suspicious accounts into rings.

◆ Decision Tiers & Plain-Language Reasons

  • ALLOW: Normal flow cleared without intervention.
  • VERIFY: Flagged for accelerated operational check.
  • HOLD: Recommended suspension pending human review.
  • Explainability: Up to 3 factual reason codes (e.g., "Passed on 94% of received funds within 14 min to 3 distinct accounts").
Human-in-the-Loop: The system only recommends actions; it never executes automated freezes without human decision.
Speaker Notes (1:30 - 2:15): Every event updates a lightweight in-memory graph. Our 59 features capture flow velocity and network structure. Ring clustering groups connected suspects, producing human-readable reasons for analysts.

Rigorous Evaluation Protocol

Methodology

◆ Controlled Benchmarks

  • ChainWorld Simulator: 60,000 customers, 3,000 merchants, 500 agents, and 140 planted campaigns across 6 typologies.
  • Strict Temporal Split: Train, Validation, Test splits split chronologically. Zero future data leakage.
  • Operating Point Hygiene: Operating thresholds fixed strictly on validation data at 0.5% target FPR, then evaluated once on test.
  • PaySim Public Benchmark: Secondary profile cross-check confirming known balance-leak caveats.

◆ 9 Leakage Controls (L1–L9)

  • L1 Temporal Integrity: Strict monotonic timestamp replay verified.
  • L3 Label-Shuffle Control: PR-AUC drops to baseline (0.0092 ≤ 0.0109 bound).
  • L4 Single-Feature Screen: No single feature dominates or leaks labels.
  • L8 Burn-In Excluded: Rolling window warm-up periods excluded from metric scoring.
  • L9 Serving Hygiene: Verification that serving engine accepts zero label fields.
Speaker Notes (2:15 - 3:00): We don't evaluate on naive random splits. We enforce strict chronological splits, fix thresholds exclusively on validation, and run 9 dedicated data leakage checks to ensure results are genuine.

IBM LinuxONE (s390x): Zero-Drift Deployment

Hardware Execution

◆ Solving the Big-Endian Byte-Order Trap

  • The Trap: Standard ML packages (numpy, scikit-learn, LightGBM) lack native s390x wheels on PyPI and standard pickles corrupt on big-endian architectures.
  • The Solution: Models are exported into portable JSON trees. A custom pure-Python tree walker evaluates inference without binary dependencies.
  • Decision Parity: < 1e-16 absolute prediction difference between x86 training and s390x serving.

◆ Measured Baseline Performance

  • Single-Worker Throughput: 1,399 events/second (benchmarked in results/bench_engine_laptop.json).
  • Median Latency (p50): 336 µs per transaction across all 59 streaming features.
  • Tail Latency (p95): 1,004 µs (p99: 2,016 µs).
  • End-to-End HTTP Latency: 7.3 ms p50 / 15.4 ms p95 at concurrency 1 (results/bench_load_laptop.json).
Honesty Rule (Spec §16.6): Decision parity (< 1e-16 delta) is verified via s390x QEMU; native LinuxONE hardware throughput is reported as pending physical instance access.
Speaker Notes (5:15 - 6:00): Deploying AI on IBM LinuxONE presents unique challenges: lack of pre-built wheels and big-endian byte ordering. We solved this with portable JSON tree evaluation. Our pure-Python engine runs at 1,399 events/second on the measured baseline with sub-millisecond median latency and zero numerical drift across architectures.

Responsible AI & Practical Limitations

Ethical Framework

◆ Ethical Considerations

  • False Alarms Harm Real People: Unwitting citizens duped into mule schemes should not be automatically cut off from banking services.
  • No Automated Blocking: ChainWatch is an advisory decision-support system. Human analysts retain final authority.
  • Neutral Terminology: Uses objective language ("account exhibits rapid-relay pattern"), avoiding stigmatizing accusations.

◆ Engineering Limits

  • Synthetic Data Caveat: Validated on synthetic streams; performance on production ledgers requires real-world bank calibration.
  • Single-Node Engine: Current state engine is single-process. Distributed scale-out would require graph partitioning or sharding.
  • No Device Signals: Operates strictly on payment events, leaving open future integration with telco/SIM-swap signals.
Speaker Notes (6:45 - 7:30): In anti-fraud AI, responsible deployment is paramount. We explicitly mandate human-in-the-loop review, avoid PII or demographic biases, and openly state that synthetic validation does not guarantee identical real-world ledgers.
